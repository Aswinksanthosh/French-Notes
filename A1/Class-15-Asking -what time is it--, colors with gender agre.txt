### Grammar: Asking "What time is it?"

| French | English |
|---|---|
| Quelle heure est-il? | What time is it? |
| Quelle heure fait-il? | What time is it? (alternate form) |

- Both mean the same thing — literal translation doesn't always work between languages (e.g. "fait" = "does," but this isn't about doing anything here).

### Grammar: Colors (les couleurs) — gender agreement

- "Couleur" is feminine. Most color adjectives change form for feminine nouns — usually by adding "e."

| Color | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Black | noir | noire |
| White | blanc | blanche |
| Green | vert | verte |
| Blue | bleu | bleue |
| Red | rouge | rouge |
| Pink | rose | rose |
| Yellow | jaune | jaune |
| Orange | orange | orange |
| Brown | marron | marron |
| Purple / Violet | violet | violette |
| Grey | gris | grise |

- une voiture noire — a black car (feminine noun → color takes "e")

### Vocabulary: word-lookalikes with "vert/verre/vers"

- Same or similar pronunciation, different meanings — context decides:
- vert = green  |  un ver de terre = earthworm  |  un verre = a glass (drinking)  |  vers = towards
- rouge à lèvres = lipstick (don't translate word-for-word: "red for lips")

### Grammar: The 5 Core Connectors (introduced here)

| Connector | Meaning | Expresses | Followed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| pour | for / in order to | the goal | verb (infinitive) or noun |
| parce que | because | the cause | full clause (subject + verb + noun) |
| à cause de | because of / due to | the cause | noun or pronoun only |
| mais | but | opposition | full clause |
| avec | with | presence | noun/pronoun |
| sans | without | absence | noun/pronoun |

- See Class 16 for more worked examples and the assignment correction on these same connectors.

- Je vais au collège pour étudier. — I go to college to study.
- L'Espagne a gagné parce qu'ils ont bien joué. — Spain won because they played well.
- Je ne suis pas venu à cause de toi. — I didn't come because of you.
- Il est drôle mais il est méchant. — He's funny but he's mean.
- Je vais au magasin avec mon ami. — I go to the shop with my friend.
- Je vais au magasin sans mon ami. — I go to the shop without my friend.

### Grammar: pour + infinitive (detail)

- Whenever pour is followed by a verb, that verb is automatically in the infinitive (unconjugated) form.
- In English, "for" is often followed by "-ing" (for studying), but in French it maps to "to + verb" (pour étudier = to study / for studying).

### Grammar: Present Continuous → "en train de"

- French has no direct equivalent of English "-ing" (present continuous). Two options:
- 1) Just use the present tense: J'étudie. = I study / I am studying.
- 2) Use "être en train de + infinitive" for extra emphasis on an action in progress: Je suis en train d'étudier. = I am (in the middle of) studying.
- Both are correct — the second is more precise/emphatic.

| French | English |
|---|---|
| je suis en train d'étudier | I'm studying |
| je suis en train de laver | I'm washing |
| je suis en train de manger | I'm eating |

### Grammar: Recognizing verb groups by infinitive ending

| Ending | Group |
|---|---|
| -er | 1st group (e.g. laver — to wash) |
| -ir | 2nd group (e.g. finir — to finish) |
| -re / -oir | 3rd group (e.g. prendre — to take) |

- The infinitive is the base/dictionary form of a verb, before it's conjugated for a subject (je, tu, il...).
