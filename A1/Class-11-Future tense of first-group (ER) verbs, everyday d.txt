*Future tense of first-group (ER) verbs, everyday documents vocabulary, and restaurant/meals vocabulary.*

### Grammar: Future tense of First Group (ER) verbs

- For the present tense, we replace or remove the -ER ending.
- For the future tense, we keep the whole infinitive (including -er) and add the ending on top: -ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont.

| habiter (to live) | manger (to eat) |
|---|---|
| j'habiterai | je mangerai |
| tu habiteras | tu mangeras |
| il/elle habitera | il/elle mangera |
| nous habiterons | nous mangerons |
| vous habiterez | vous mangerez |
| ils/elles habiteront | ils/elles mangeront |

- Je mangerai du riz aujourd'hui. — I will eat rice today.

### Grammar: Appeler in the future tense (doubled consonant)

- Appeler doubles its "l" throughout the future tense (and present tense) — j'appellerai, not j'appelerai.

| Future | Present (for comparison) |
|---|---|
| j'appellerai | j'appelle |
| tu appelleras | tu appelles |
| il/elle appellera | il/elle appelle |
| nous appellerons | nous appelons |
| vous appellerez | vous appelez |
| ils/elles appelleront | ils/elles appellent |

### Classroom Discussion & Q&A

- **Future tense — the core rule, contrasted explicitly against the present tense:** the instructor was careful to state the mechanical difference clearly: present tense replaces/removes -er; future tense keeps the whole infinitive and adds an ending on top. A student asked to confirm this was really a different rule, not just a special case, and the instructor confirmed it applies across the whole first group.
- **appeler in the future — why it doubles its L, and where exactly:** a student pushed on this in detail, asking whether the double-L applies to every form. The instructor mapped it out precisely: at the present tense, double-L appears at je/tu/il/ils forms but single-L at nous/vous; at the future tense, double-L appears in every single form (j'appellerai, tu appelleras, etc.) — flagged as something to just memorize since there's no deeper phonetic rule explaining the pattern, only a personal memory trick the instructor shared (associating double-L with a stressed "appelle" sound vs single-L with an unstressed one).
- **Testing informal texting shorthand (j'appell' instead of j'appellerai) — instructor firmly discourages it:** a student asked whether dropping a vowel + adding an apostrophe (the way "you're" replaces "you are" casually in English) works generally in French. Confirmed: it exists but is very informal (SMS-speak between close friends) and the instructor explicitly recommends against using it, especially since it doesn't apply universally — you can't just apostrophize any vowel-ending word.
- **il/elle appellent — why the final "ent" is silent, tied back to an English-vs-French contrast:** a student connected this to English words like "comment," where the final consonant cluster IS pronounced, and asked whether French always drops it. The instructor clarified this isn't a universal spelling rule but specific to verb endings — the -ent ending on 3rd-person-plural verbs is consistently silent across all verb groups, which is different from unrelated words that happen to end in similar letters.
- **Third-group future stems are irregular and don't follow one universal pattern — demonstrated, then explicitly walked back to avoid over-generalizing:** the instructor showed prendre (je prendrai) and vouloir (je voudrai — stem changes from vouloir to voudr-) as two "third group" verbs that don't share the same future-stem transformation, specifically to head off a student's assumption that all -DRE-ending third-group verbs behave identically. Confirmed only vendre-type -DRE verbs are that consistent; vouloir is its own irregular case.
- **passer — another verb whose meaning shifts entirely with context, similar to faire/de earlier:** a student asked whether passer always implies literally handing something to someone. The instructor confirmed no — it can mean "to pass [an object]" or "to pass [an exam]" depending on context, reinforcing the running theme that many French verbs carry multiple English translations depending on what follows them.
