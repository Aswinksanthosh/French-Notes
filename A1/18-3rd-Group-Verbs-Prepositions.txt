### Grammar: Vendre (3rd group) — "to sell" — present tense

| Pronoun | Conjugation |
|---|---|
| je | vends |
| tu | vends |
| il / elle | vend |
| nous | vendons |
| vous | vendez |
| ils / elles | vendent |

- Je vends mon stylo à mon ami. — I sell my pen to my friend.
- Je vends des légumes à mes parents. — I sell some vegetables to my parents.

### Grammar: Second Group (IR) verbs — the two conditions

- Condition 1: the verb's infinitive ends in -IR.
- Condition 2: its present participle ends in -ISSANT.
- finir (to finish) → finissant (finishing)
- choisir (to choose) → choisissant (choosing)

| finir (to finish) | choisir (to choose) |
|---|---|
| je finis | je choisis |
| tu finis | tu choisis |
| il/elle finit | il/elle choisit |
| nous finissons | nous choisissons |
| vous finissez | vous choisissez |
| ils/elles finissent | ils/elles choisissent |

### Grammar: The present participle rule (-ANT vs -ISSANT)

- The present participle of almost every French verb ends in -ANT (1st group and 3rd group alike).
- The only exception is second-group (-IR / -ISSANT) verbs — their present participle ends in -ISSANT instead. This is actually how you can double-check a verb is second group.

| Verb | Group | Present participle |
|---|---|---|
| aller — to go | irregular (3rd) | en allant |
| manger — to eat | 1st | en mangeant |
| parler — to speak | 1st | en parlant |
| marcher — to walk | 1st | en marchant |
| partir — to leave | 3rd | partant |
| finir — to finish | 2nd | finissant |
| choisir — to choose | 2nd | choisissant |

### Grammar: Expressing an ongoing action

- French has no direct "-ing" present continuous tense. Two ways to express it:
- 1) être en train de + infinitive — emphasizes the action is happening right now.
- 2) Simply use the present tense — often just as natural.

- I'm going to school — je suis en train d'aller à l'école, or simply je vais à l'école.
- She is finishing her homework — elle est en train de finir son devoir, or simply elle finit son devoir.

### Classroom Discussion & Q&A

- **Why reading matters even for speaking — a direct answer to "does reading actually help?":** the instructor's case: reading correctly out loud is functionally close to speaking (same pronunciation muscles, same confidence-building), and reading also trains recognizing words fast — both feed into listening and speaking ability rather than being a separate isolated skill.
- **vendre — why it's third group, drilled with a direct why-question:** a student was asked to identify the group and explain why; the class walked through it together: ends in -DRE, which is a third-group ending (not first -ER or second -IR), so it follows irregular third-group conjugation (je vends, tu vends, il vend — note no -e/-es/-e pattern at all, and vend takes a "d" not a silent letter).
- **mon ami vs mon amie — the same vowel-exception drilled again, but now inside a full sentence:** "je vends mon stylo à mon ami/amie" was used to re-confirm that even mid-sentence, feminine nouns starting with a vowel still take mon (not ma) — reinforcing this rule now shows up constantly, not just in isolated vocabulary drills.
- **légumes — indefinite plural again, applied to a fresh example:** "je vends des légumes" was built step by step to show indefinite plural (des) applies the same way it did for definite articles earlier — quantity unknown + plural noun = des, regardless of the noun's gender.
- **mettre — a second irregular third-group verb, immediately contrasted with vendre to show they don't share a pattern:** je mets, tu mets, il met — different irregular shape from vendre despite both being third group, reinforcing that "third group" is really a catch-all for verbs that don't follow first/second group rules, not a single shared pattern.
- **sous vs moins — a subtle contrast a student pushed hard to understand:** the class worked through why "j'aime vendre la voiture sous 5000 euros" is wrong — sous means physically "under" (spatially beneath something, like under a bridge), whereas expressing a price ceiling ("for under 5000 euros") needs à moins de ("for less than"). A student explicitly asked for a physical vs. price-context comparison, which the instructor gave directly (la voiture est sous le pont vs. à moins de 5000 euros) to make the contrast concrete.
- **en vente vs à vendre — two equally valid ways to say "for sale," confirmed as interchangeable:** a student asked whether both were correct; instructor confirmed yes, no meaningful difference in this context.
- **Second vs third group verbs ending in -IR — the same core distinction from Class 9 revisited, but the class realized they hadn't fully covered it yet:** this class started introducing venir ("to come") as the second-group test case, but the instructor and students both realized mid-discussion that the deeper second-vs-third-group distinction (the -issant present participle test) hadn't actually been taught yet in a prior class as assumed — flagged honestly as a gap to properly cover next time rather than glossed over.
