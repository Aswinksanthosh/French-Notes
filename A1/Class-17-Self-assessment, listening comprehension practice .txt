### Grammar: Adjective Agreement (suivant / suivante)

- The adjective agrees in gender with the noun it describes.
- l'annonce suivante — "annonce" is feminine → adjective takes an "e"
- l'animal suivant — "animal" is masculine → no "e"
- la voiture suivante — "voiture" (car) is feminine → takes "e"

### Grammar: quel vs qu'est-ce que

- Quel = "which" or "what" — used with a noun. Example: Quel jour? (Which day?)
- Qu'est-ce que = "what" — used when asking about an action/thing more generally. Example: Qu'est-ce que vous devez apporter? (What do you need to bring?)

### Grammar: chez + person

| French | Meaning |
|---|---|
| chez moi | at my place |
| chez toi | at your place |
| chez lui | at his place |
| chez elle | at her place |
| chez nous | at our place |
| chez vous | at your place (plural/formal) |
| chez eux | at their place (masc./mixed group) |
| chez elles | at their place (all female group) |

- Note: there is no "chez il" — the correct form is "chez lui."

### Grammar: devoir (to have to) — present tense

| Pronoun | Conjugation |
|---|---|
| je | dois |
| tu | dois |
| il / elle | doit |
| nous | devons |
| vous | devez |
| ils / elles | doivent |

- Je dois aller à la maison. — I have to go home.
- Je dois manger. — I have to eat.
- Je dois parler français. — I have to speak French.

- Devoir is an irregular (3rd group) verb — doesn't follow the standard ER/IR patterns.

### Grammar: Passé Composé (intro)

- J'ai mangé. — I have eaten. (Passé composé = present perfect in English)

### Grammar: Question formation (inversion)

- Manges-tu? = Tu manges? — "Are you eating?" (verb-first = question, even without "?")

### Classroom Discussion & Q&A

- **Self-assessment moment that opened the class:** students were asked to rate their own speaking (most rated themselves below 1/10) and listening (2–3/10) — the instructor used this to introduce why the class was pivoting to focused listening practice, framing low self-ratings as normal at this stage rather than a problem.
- **"How do I practice speaking if I can't build my own sentences yet?" — a direct student question, answered at length:** the instructor's core answer was that speaking depends on already knowing the building blocks (subject–verb–object, the verb conjugated correctly for the tense, and enough vocabulary for the day-to-day scenario) — comparing it to needing all the ingredients before you can cook a dish. Emphasized that memorizing correctly, especially verbs, isn't a shortcut around understanding grammar — both work together.
- **Why the ER verb list keeps coming up as "non-negotiable":** the instructor repeated, more forcefully than before, that a random 10-verb recall test could happen any class, and that mastering these verbs is what unlocks being able to describe almost any daily scenario (going to the library, the supermarket, etc.) without needing new vocabulary each time.
- **qu'est-ce que vs que vs quel — a student specifically asked for the difference, and the instructor promised (but didn't fully deliver) a structured answer:** flagged as something with real nuance the instructor wanted to explain properly with reference material rather than off the cuff — a partial answer was given (quel relates to a noun/"which," que/qu'est-ce que relates more to actions) but the instructor explicitly deferred a full explanation to a future class.
- **suivant vs suivante — practiced live across several nouns to nail the masculine/feminine agreement pattern:** l'annonce suivante (feminine — announce is feminine) vs l'animal suivant (masculine — animal is masculine) were worked side by side specifically so students could see the same adjective changing form based purely on the noun's gender, not on any property of "next" itself.
- **mange-tu? vs tu manges — question formation via inversion, explained through a concrete pair:** the instructor showed that swapping the verb before the pronoun (mange-tu?) turns a statement into a question without needing a question mark to be obvious in speech — contrasted with tu manges (statement) to make the word-order signal explicit.
- **J'ai mangé — introduced for the first time as passé composé, deliberately distinguished from je dois manger:** a student asked whether "j'ai mangé" and "je dois manger" were interchangeable. The instructor clarified they are structurally unrelated: je dois manger uses devoir + infinitive ("I have to eat"), while j'ai mangé is the passé composé (roughly English present perfect — "I have eaten," a completed action) — introduced here as a preview, not something to master yet.
- **chez moi / chez toi / chez lui / chez elle / chez eux / chez elles — the full chez set, explicitly compared to a pronoun rather than a preposition + noun:** the instructor flagged these words after chez (moi, toi, lui, elle, eux, elles) as their own special category of pronoun, separate from subject pronouns like je/tu/il — introduced as vocabulary to memorize as fixed set phrases rather than something built compositionally.
- **Exam timeline discussion — logistics, not language, but flagged for context:** students asked directly when the A1 exam would be. The instructor worked it out live with the class by counting remaining sessions (roughly 13 classes left at the time), estimating the exam for around September. Confirmed the internal A1 "exam" isn't pass/fail in the way a formal certification test would be — everyone who takes it and puts in effort will receive a certificate of participation, and grades don't block moving on to A2.
