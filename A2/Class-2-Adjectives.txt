French Adjectives & Adverbs - A2 Class 2

PART 1: LES ADJECTIFS EN FRANÇAIS
Adjectives: Forms, Position & Function

1. WHAT IS AN ADJECTIVE?

Français: Un adjectif est un mot qui décrit ou précise un nom. Il peut exprimer une qualité, une couleur, une taille, une forme, une opinion, etc.

English: An adjective is a word that describes or gives information about a noun. It can express a quality, color, size, shape, opinion, etc.

Examples:
- une grande maison (a big house)
- un homme gentil (a kind man)
- des voitures rapides (fast cars)

Main function: The adjective gives information about the noun.

2. ADJECTIVE AGREEMENT (L'ACCORD DE L'ADJECTIF)

The adjective agrees with the noun in GENDER (masculine/feminine) and NUMBER (singular/plural).

| Form | Example | Translation |
|------|---------|-------------|
| Masculine singular | un homme grand | a tall man |
| Feminine singular | une femme grande | a tall woman |
| Masculine plural | des hommes grands | tall men |
| Feminine plural | des femmes grandes | tall women |

3. FORMING THE FEMININE

General rule: Add -e to the masculine adjective

Examples:
- grand → grande (big)
- petit → petite (small)
- lent → lente (slow)
- vert → verte (green)
- intelligent → intelligente (intelligent)

Important: If the masculine already ends in -e, the feminine form remains the same
- un homme calme → une femme calme (a calm man → a calm woman)

4. COMMON FEMININE CHANGES

Pattern: -f → -ve
- actif → active (active)
- sportif → sportive (sporty)
- neuf → neuve (new)

Pattern: -x → -se
- heureux → heureuse (happy)
- dangereux → dangereuse (dangerous)
- sérieux → sérieuse (serious)

Pattern: -er → -ère
- cher → chère (expensive/dear)
- premier → première (first)
- léger → légère (light)

Pattern: -eux → -euse
- heureux → heureuse (happy)
- curieux → curieuse (curious)
- courageux → courageuse (brave)

Pattern: -if → -ive
- créatif → créative (creative)
- attentif → attentive (attentive)
- sportif → sportive (sporty)

Pattern: -ien → -ienne
- ancien → ancienne (old/former)
- européen → européenne (European)
- musicien → musicienne (musician)

Pattern: -el → -elle
- cruel → cruelle (cruel)
- naturel → naturelle (natural)
- actuel → actuelle (current)

Pattern: -on → -onne
- bon → bonne (good)
- mignon → mignonne (cute)
- ancien → ancienne (old/former)

Pattern: -et → -ette
- net → nette (clear/neat)
- discret → discrète (discreet)
- inquiet → inquiète (worried)

Irregular forms (learn individually):
- beau → belle (beautiful/handsome)
- nouveau → nouvelle (new)
- vieux → vieille (old)
- blanc → blanche (white)
- frais → fraîche (fresh)
- doux → douce (soft/sweet)

5. FORMING THE PLURAL

General rule: Add -s to the adjective

Examples:
- grand → grands
- grande → grandes
- petit → petits
- petite → petites
- rapide → rapides

Sentences:
- un garçon intelligent → des garçons intelligents (an intelligent boy → intelligent boys)
- une fille intelligente → des filles intelligentes (an intelligent girl → intelligent girls)

Special cases:

Adjectives ending in -s or -x: Generally remain unchanged
- un homme heureux → des hommes heureux (a happy man → happy men)
- une femme heureuse → des femmes heureuses (a happy woman → happy women)

Adjectives ending in -al → -aux (for certain adjectives):
- national → nationaux (national)
- original → originaux (original)
- général → généraux (general)

6. ADJECTIVES BEFORE THE NOUN

Most descriptive adjectives come AFTER the noun. However, some very common adjectives usually come BEFORE it.

Adjectives that come BEFORE the noun (BAGS: Beauty, Age, Goodness, Size):

Beauty: beau, joli (beautiful, pretty)
Age: jeune, vieux, nouveau (young, old, new)
Goodness: bon, mauvais, meilleur (good, bad, better)
Size: grand, petit, gros, long, court (big, small, fat, long, short)

Examples BEFORE:
- un petit garçon (a small boy)
- une grande maison (a big house)
- un beau film (a beautiful/good film)
- une jeune femme (a young woman)

Remember: This is NOT an absolute rule. There are exceptions that must be learned individually.

7. ADJECTIVES AFTER THE NOUN

Color, nationality, shape, material, and many descriptive adjectives usually come AFTER the noun.

Examples AFTER:
- une voiture rouge (a red car)
- un étudiant français (a French student)
- une table ronde (a round table)
- une chemise blanche (a white shirt)
- un problème difficile (a difficult problem)
- une histoire intéressante (an interesting story)
- un homme courageux (a courageous man)

8. WHY POSITION MATTERS - ADJECTIVE POSITION CHANGES MEANING

Position can be linked to grammar, but it can also change the meaning or nuance of the sentence.

Examples:
- un ancien professeur = a former teacher (old job)
- un professeur ancien = an old/ancient teacher (age)

- un grand homme = a great man (importance/character)
- un homme grand = a tall man (height/physical)

- un pauvre homme = poor/unfortunate man (feeling sorry)
- un homme pauvre = a poor man financially (lacking money)

- une seule personne = one/only person (quantity)
- une personne seule = a person who is alone (solitary/isolation)

Important rule: The position of an adjective is not just a matter of grammatical position; it can modify the sense/meaning.

9. COLOR ADJECTIVES (ADJECTIFS DE COULEUR)

Most color adjectives agree with the noun:
- un pantalon noir → une veste noire → des pantalons noirs → des vestes noires
- un sac vert → une voiture verte → des sacs verts → des voitures vertes

Colors from nouns (Couleurs provenant de noms): orange, marron are generally INVARIABLE (don't change)
- une robe orange → des robes orange (an orange dress → orange dresses)
- un pantalon marron → des pantalons marron (brown pants → brown pants)

10. SEVERAL ADJECTIVES WITH ONE NOUN

All adjectives agree with the noun they describe:
- une grande et belle maison (a big and beautiful house)
- de petits restaurants italiens (small Italian restaurants)
- des voitures rapides et confortables (fast and comfortable cars)
- un homme intelligent, calme et patient (an intelligent, calm and patient man)

11. PREDICATE ADJECTIVE (L'ADJECTIF ATTRIBUT)

After certain verbs like être, sembler, paraître, devenir, rester, the adjective can be separated from the noun. It still describes the subject.

Verbs that separate adjectives:
- être (to be)
- sembler (to seem)
- paraître (to appear)
- devenir (to become)
- rester (to remain)

Examples:
- La maison est grande. (The house is big.)
- Les enfants sont heureux. (The children are happy.)
- Marie semble fatiguée. (Marie seems tired.)
- Le projet devient intéressant. (The project is becoming interesting.)

12. ATTRIBUTIVE vs PREDICATE ADJECTIVES

ATTRIBUTIVE (Épithète): The adjective directly accompanies the noun
- une voiture rapide (a fast car)
- un joli jardin (a pretty garden)
- un vélo blanc (a white bicycle)

PREDICATE (Attribut): The adjective is linked to the subject by a verb
- La voiture est rapide. (The car is fast.)
- Le jardin est joli. (The garden is pretty.)
- Le vélo est blanc. (The bicycle is white.)

In both cases, the adjective describes the noun, but its grammatical position/function is different.

13. ADJECTIVES USED AS NOUNS

An adjective can sometimes be used as a noun, often with an article:
- les riches (the rich)
- les pauvres (the poor)
- un jeune (a young person/man)
- une jeune (a young woman/person)

14. KEY RULES TO REMEMBER

1. AGREEMENT: The adjective agrees with the noun in gender and number.

2. FEMININE: Often masculine + -e, but many changes and exceptions exist.

3. PLURAL: Generally add -s.

4. BEFORE: Many beauty, age, goodness and size adjectives often come before the noun.

5. AFTER: Colors, nationalities and many descriptions usually come after the noun.

6. MEANING: Position can sometimes change the meaning or nuance.

FINAL TIP: Learn adjectives with their four forms when they exist: grand, grande, grands, grandes. Then practise placing them correctly in sentences.

PART 2: LES ADVERBES EN FRANÇAIS
Adverbs: Functions, Uses & Formation - Time, Place, Manner

1. WHAT IS AN ADVERB?

Français: Un adverbe est un mot invariable qui sert principalement à préciser le sens d'un verbe, d'un adjectif, d'un autre adverbe ou parfois de toute une phrase.

English: An adverb is an invariable word that mainly gives more information about a verb, an adjective, another adverb, or sometimes a whole sentence.

IMPORTANT: An adverb does not agree in gender or number; it does not change.

Examples:
- Il parle lentement. (He speaks slowly.)
- Elle est très gentille. (She is very kind.)
- Il court vraiment vite. (He runs really fast.)

2. FUNCTIONS OF ADVERBS

1. MODIFY A VERB: Elle chante magnifiquement. (She sings beautifully.)

2. MODIFY AN ADJECTIVE: Il est très intelligent. (He is very intelligent.)

3. MODIFY ANOTHER ADVERB: Elle parle assez rapidement. (She speaks quite quickly.)

4. MODIFY THE WHOLE SENTENCE: Malheureusement, il est absent. (Unfortunately, he is absent.)

3. MAIN TYPES OF ADVERBS

Adverbes de manière (Manner): Comment? — How?
- lentement (slowly)
- rapidement (quickly)
- bien (well)
- mal (badly)
- facilement (easily)

Adverbes de temps (Time): Quand? — When?
- aujourd'hui (today)
- hier (yesterday)
- demain (tomorrow)
- bientôt (soon)
- toujours (always)

Adverbes de lieu (Place): Où? — Where?
- ici (here)
- là (there)
- ailleurs (elsewhere)
- partout (everywhere)
- dehors (outside)

Adverbes de quantité/intensité (Degree): Combien? — How much?
- très (very)
- beaucoup (a lot)
- peu (little)
- assez (enough/quite)
- trop (too much)

Adverbes de fréquence (Frequency): À quelle fréquence? — How often?
- toujours (always)
- souvent (often)
- parfois (sometimes)
- rarement (rarely)
- jamais (never)

4. FORMING ADVERBS IN -MENT

Many manner adverbs are formed from adjectives and end in -ment.

Basic pattern: Take the feminine form of the adjective and add -ment

Examples:
- lent → lente → lentement (slow → slowly)
- rapide → rapidement (quick → quickly) [no feminine -e needed]
- heureux → heureuse → heureusement (happy → happily)
- sérieux → sérieusement (serious → seriously) [no feminine -e]

More examples:
- général → généralement (generally)
- naturel → naturellement (naturally)
- facile → facilement (easily)

Practical rule: For many adjectives ending in -e, add -ment directly:
- simple → simplement (simply)
- vrai → vraiment (truly/really)

5. MANNER ADVERBS (ADVERBES DE MANIÈRE)

Question: Comment? — How?

Common manner adverbs:
- bien (well)
- mal (badly)
- vite (quickly)
- lentement (slowly)
- doucement (gently/softly)
- fort (loudly/hard)
- facilement (easily)
- difficilement (with difficulty)

Sentences:
- Il travaille sérieusement. (He works seriously.)
- Elle répond poliment. (She answers politely.)
- Ils ont terminé le travail rapidement. (They finished the work quickly.)
- Parle doucement, s'il te plaît. (Speak softly, please.)
- Il conduit prudemment. (He drives carefully.)

6. TIME ADVERBS (ADVERBES DE TEMPS)

Question: Quand? — When?

Common time adverbs:
- aujourd'hui (today)
- hier (yesterday)
- avant-hier (the day before yesterday)
- demain (tomorrow)
- après-demain (the day after tomorrow)
- maintenant (now)
- bientôt (soon)
- tard (late)
- tôt (early)
- déjà (already)
- encore (still/again)
- toujours (always/still)
- jamais (never)
- autrefois (formerly)
- récemment (recently)
- prochainement (soon/in the near future)
- actuellement (currently)
- immédiatement (immediately)
- ensuite (afterwards/then)
- enfin (finally)

Sentences:
- Je travaille aujourd'hui. (I am working today.)
- Nous sommes arrivés hier. (We arrived yesterday.)
- Elle partira demain. (She will leave tomorrow.)
- Il a déjà mangé. (He has already eaten.)
- Je reviendrai bientôt. (I will come back soon.)
- Ils vivent encore ici. (They still live here.)
- Nous avons enfin trouvé une solution. (We finally found a solution.)

7. PLACE ADVERBS (ADVERBES DE LIEU)

Question: Où? — Where?

Common place adverbs:
- ici (here)
- là (there)
- là-bas (over there)
- ailleurs (elsewhere)
- partout (everywhere)
- nulle part (nowhere)
- quelque part (somewhere)
- dessus (on top/above)
- dessous (underneath/below)
- dedans (inside)
- dehors (outside)
- devant (in front)
- derrière (behind)
- près (near)
- loin (far)
- autour (around)
- en haut (upstairs/above)
- en bas (downstairs/below)

Sentences:
- Viens ici. (Come here.)
- Il habite ailleurs. (He lives elsewhere.)
- Les enfants jouent dehors. (The children are playing outside.)
- J'ai cherché partout. (I searched everywhere.)
- Il n'est nulle part. (He is nowhere.)
- Le chat est dessous. (The cat is underneath.)
- Elle habite loin. (She lives far away.)

8. ADVERBS OF QUANTITY AND INTENSITY

They show degree or quantity:
- très (very)
- trop (too much/too)
- assez (enough/quite)
- beaucoup (a lot)
- peu (little)
- plus (more)
- moins (less)
- presque (almost)
- tellement (so much/so)
- vraiment (really)
- complètement (completely)
- particulièrement (particularly)

Sentences:
- Elle est très intelligente. (She is very intelligent.)
- Il travaille beaucoup. (He works a lot.)
- C'est trop cher. (It is too expensive.)
- Nous sommes presque prêts. (We are almost ready.)

9. FREQUENCY ADVERBS (ADVERBES DE FRÉQUENCE)

- toujours (always)
- souvent (often)
- généralement (generally)
- habituellement (usually)
- normalement (normally)
- parfois (sometimes)
- quelquefois (sometimes)
- rarement (rarely)
- presque jamais (almost never)
- jamais (never)

Sentences:
- Je vais souvent au marché. (I often go to the market.)
- Elle est toujours ponctuelle. (She is always punctual.)
- Nous mangeons rarement au restaurant. (We rarely eat at a restaurant.)
- Il ne fume jamais. (He never smokes.)

ATTENTION: jamais is often used with ne in a negative sentence
- Je ne viens jamais. (I never come.)

10. POSITION OF ADVERBS

With a simple verb: The adverb usually comes after the verb
- Il parle lentement. (He speaks slowly.)
- Elle travaille beaucoup. (She works a lot.)

With the passé composé (compound past): Short and frequent adverbs are often placed between the auxiliary and the past participle
- J'ai déjà mangé. (I have already eaten.)
- Il a bien travaillé. (He worked well.)
- Elle est souvent venue ici. (She has often come here.)

But: Long adverbs or certain manner adverbs are often placed after the past participle
- Il a répondu rapidement. (He answered quickly.)
- Elle a travaillé sérieusement. (She worked seriously.)

11. BEFORE AN ADJECTIVE

Degree adverbs usually come before the adjective:
- très beau (very beautiful)
- vraiment important (really important)
- assez facile (quite easy)
- trop difficile (too difficult)

12. BEFORE ANOTHER ADVERB

An adverb can modify another adverb:
- Il parle très lentement. (He speaks very slowly.)
- Elle travaille assez rapidement. (She works quite quickly.)
- Il court vraiment vite. (He runs really fast.)

13. DO NOT CONFUSE ADJECTIVE AND ADVERB

ADJECTIVE describes a noun:
- une voiture rapide (a fast car)

ADVERB describes a verb (and other words):
- elle conduit rapidement (she drives quickly)

Compare:
- un homme heureux (a happy man) — ADJECTIVE
- il sourit heureusement (he smiles happily) — ADVERB

14. INTERROGATIVE ADVERBS

- où? (where?)
- quand? (when?)
- comment? (how?)
- pourquoi? (why?)
- combien? (how much/how many?)

Sentences:
- Où habites-tu? (Where do you live?)
- Quand arrives-tu? (When are you arriving?)
- Comment vas-tu? (How are you?)
- Pourquoi pars-tu? (Why are you leaving?)
- Combien ça coûte? (How much does it cost?)

15. LINKING ADVERBS (ADVERBES DE LIAISON)

They organize discourse:
- cependant (however)
- pourtant (nevertheless)
- donc (therefore/so)
- ainsi (thus)
- ensuite (then)
- enfin (finally)
- également (also)
- malheureusement (unfortunately)
- heureusement (fortunately)

Sentences:
- Malheureusement, nous avons raté le train. (Unfortunately, we missed the train.)
- Heureusement, personne n'a été blessé. (Fortunately, nobody was injured.)
- Cependant, il a continué. (However, he continued.)

16. SUMMARY LISTS

TEMPS (TIME): aujourd'hui, hier, demain, avant-hier, après-demain, maintenant, bientôt, tard, tôt, déjà, encore, toujours, jamais, autrefois, récemment, actuellement, immédiatement, ensuite, enfin, finalement.

LIEU (PLACE): ici, là, là-bas, ailleurs, partout, nulle part, quelque part, dessus, dessous, dedans, dehors, devant, derrière, près, loin, autour, en haut, en bas.

MANIÈRE (MANNER): bien, mal, vite, lentement, doucement, fort, facilement, difficilement, rapidement, correctement, poliment, sérieusement, prudemment, clairement, simplement, naturellement, heureusement.

QUANTITÉ (DEGREE): très, trop, assez, beaucoup, peu, plus, moins, presque, tellement, vraiment, complètement, particulièrement, davantage, suffisamment.

FRÉQUENCE (FREQUENCY): toujours, souvent, généralement, habituellement, normalement, parfois, quelquefois, rarement, presque jamais, jamais.

17. MODEL SENTENCES B1–B2 LEVEL

1. Habituellement, je me lève tôt parce que je travaille loin de chez moi. — Usually, I get up early because I work far from home.

2. Elle a déjà terminé son projet, mais elle veut encore l'améliorer. — She has already finished her project, but she still wants to improve it.

3. Nous avons heureusement trouvé une solution assez rapidement. — Fortunately, we found a solution quite quickly.

4. Il parle très clairement, donc tout le monde comprend facilement. — He speaks very clearly, so everyone understands easily.

5. Je ne vais presque jamais là-bas parce que c'est trop loin. — I almost never go there because it is too far.

6. Malheureusement, elle est arrivée trop tard pour prendre le train. — Unfortunately, she arrived too late to catch the train.

7. Les étudiants travaillent sérieusement et progressent rapidement. — The students work seriously and improve quickly.

8. Il a vraiment beaucoup travaillé pour réussir son examen. — He really worked a lot to pass his exam.

18. KEY POINTS TO REMEMBER

An adverb is invariable: It does not change for gender or number.

It gives more information: About a verb, adjective, another adverb, or an entire sentence.

TEMPS (Time): quand? — aujourd'hui, hier, demain, bientôt…

LIEU (Place): où? — ici, là-bas, ailleurs, partout…

MANIÈRE (Manner): comment? — bien, mal, lentement, rapidement…

FRÉQUENCE (Frequency): à quelle fréquence? — toujours, souvent, parfois, rarement, jamais

POSITION (Position): Often after the verb, but position varies depending on the type of adverb and verbal tense.

CONSEIL (TIP): For better progress, learn each adverb together with a complete sentence.
