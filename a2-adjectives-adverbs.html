📚 CLASS 1: LES ADJECTIFS EN FRANÇAIS (French Adjectives)

Introduction

An adjective is a word that describes, qualifies, or provides information about a noun. It can express quality, color, size, form, or opinion. Adjectives are one of the most important parts of speech in French because they help us describe people, things, and situations precisely.

🔑 KEY POINT: Adjectives MUST AGREE with the noun they describe in both GENDER and NUMBER. This means if the noun is masculine singular, the adjective must be masculine singular. If the noun is feminine plural, the adjective must be feminine plural.

PART 1: ADJECTIVE AGREEMENT

Rule 1: Basic Gender Agreement

Add an -E to the masculine form to make it feminine:

  • grand → grande (big/tall)
  • petit → petite (small)
  • intelligent → intelligente (intelligent)
  • lent → lente (slow)
  • vert → verte (green)

Rule 2: Adjectives Already Ending in -E

If the masculine form already ends in -e, the feminine form stays the same:

  • calme → calme (calm)
  • facile → facile (easy)
  • difficile → difficile (difficult)

Rule 3: Special Feminine Formations

-F changes to -VE:

  • actif → active (active)
  • sportif → sportive (sporty)
  • créatif → créative (creative)
  • attentif → attentive (attentive)

-X changes to -SE:

  • heureux → heureuse (happy)
  • dangereux → dangereuse (dangerous)
  • curieux → curieuse (curious)
  • courageux → courageuse (courageous)
  • sérieux → sérieuse (serious)

-ER changes to -ÈRE:

  • cher → chère (dear/expensive)
  • premier → première (first)
  • léger → légère (light)

-IEN changes to -IENNE:

  • ancien → ancienne (old/former)
  • européen → européenne (European)
  • musicien → musicienne (musical)

-ON changes to -ONNE:

  • bon → bonne (good)
  • mignon → mignonne (cute)

Rule 4: Irregular Adjectives (Learn These!)

These don't follow regular patterns:

  • beau → belle (beautiful)
  • nouveau → nouvelle (new)
  • vieux → vieille (old)
  • blanc → blanche (white)
  • frais → fraîche (fresh)
  • doux → douce (sweet/soft)

Rule 5: Plural Formation

General rule: Add -S to singular adjectives:

  • grand → grands
  • grande → grandes
  • petit → petits
  • petite → petites

Adjectives ending in -s or -x remain unchanged:

  • heureux (singular masculine) = heureux (plural masculine)
  • heureux (singular feminine) = heureuses (plural feminine)

Adjectives ending in -AL change to -AUX:

  • national → nationaux
  • original → originaux
  • général → généraux

PART 2: ADJECTIVE PLACEMENT

Rule 1: Most Adjectives Come AFTER the Noun

Colors, nationalities, shapes, materials, and descriptive adjectives generally follow the noun:

une voiture rouge (a red car)
un étudiant français (a French student)
une table ronde (a round table)
une chemise blanche (a white shirt)
un problème difficile (a difficult problem)

Rule 2: Some Common Adjectives Come BEFORE the Noun

Adjectives relating to beauty, age, goodness, and size often come before the noun:

un petit garçon (a small boy)
une grande maison (a big house)
un beau film (a beautiful movie)
une jeune femme (a young woman)
un bon ami (a good friend)
un mauvais jour (a bad day)

Remember: This is a guideline, not an absolute rule. There are exceptions.

Rule 3: Position Can Change Meaning!

Some adjectives have different meanings depending on their placement:

  • un ancien professeur = a former teacher (professeur who is no longer teaching)
  • un professeur ancien = an old teacher (professeur who is elderly)
  • un grand homme = a great man (homme with important qualities)
  • un homme grand = a tall man (homme who has height)
  • un pauvre homme = an unfortunate man (homme to be pitied)
  • un homme pauvre = a poor man (homme lacking money)

PART 3: SPECIAL FORMS FOR MASCULINE BEFORE VOWELS

Three adjectives have special masculine singular forms used before masculine nouns beginning with a vowel or silent H:

beau → bel (before vowel/silent H)
un bel homme (a handsome man)
un bel oiseau (a beautiful bird)

nouveau → nouvel (before vowel/silent H)
un nouvel abonnement (a new subscription)

vieux → vieil (before vowel/silent H)
un vieil homme (an old man)

⚠️ These are the ONLY three adjectives with this special form. Remember them!

PART 4: EPITHÈTE VS ATTRIBUT (Two Types of Adjectives)

Adjective Épithète (Epithet Adjective)

Directly accompanies the noun. Can be before or after the noun. No verb separating the adjective from the noun.

une voiture rapide (a fast car)
un garçon intelligent (an intelligent boy)
un petit chat (a small cat)
le joli jardin (the pretty garden)
un vélo blanc (a white bicycle)

Adjective Attribut (Attributive Adjective)

Separated from the noun by a linking verb. Still agrees with the subject. Common linking verbs: être (to be), sembler (to seem), paraître (to appear), devenir (to become).

La voiture est rapide (The car is fast)
Le garçon est intelligent (The boy is intelligent)
Le chat est petit (The cat is small)
Le jardin est joli (The garden is pretty)
Le vélo est blanc (The bicycle is white)

Important: Both convey the same information but are used in different sentence structures. Epithet: "The intelligent boy" (adjective directly with noun). Attribut: "The boy is intelligent" (adjective after verb).

PART 5: ADJECTIVES USED AS NOUNS

Sometimes adjectives can be used as nouns with an article:

les riches = the rich (people)
les pauvres = the poor (people)
un jeune = a young person
une jeune = a young woman

PART 6: COLOR ADJECTIVES

Agreement Rule

Color adjectives agree with the noun in gender and number:

un sac vert = a green bag
une voiture verte = a green car
des sacs verts = green bags
des voitures vertes = green cars

Exception: Colors from Nouns

Colors derived from nouns (orange, marron) are INVARIABLE (don't change):

des robes orange = orange dresses
un pantalon marron = brown pants
des pantalons marrons = brown pants

📚 CLASS 2: LES ADVERBES EN FRANÇAIS (French Adverbs)

Introduction

An adverb is an invariable word (it never changes) that serves to modify or clarify:

  • a verb (showing HOW an action is done)
  • an adjective (showing the degree or intensity)
  • another adverb (modifying another adverb)
  • sometimes an entire sentence (expressing opinion about the whole sentence)

Unlike adjectives, adverbs do NOT agree with nouns in gender or number.

🔑 KEY DIFFERENCE:
Adjective: Describes or qualifies a NOUN
Adverb: Modifies a VERB, ADJECTIVE, ADVERB, or SENTENCE

Quick Test

After a verb, ask yourself: "Does this word modify the noun or the action?" If it modifies the action/verb, it's an adverb.

PART 1: FUNCTIONS OF ADVERBS

Function 1: Modifying a Verb

Adverbs of manner show HOW an action is performed:

Elle chante magnifiquement = She sings beautifully
Il parle lentement = He speaks slowly
Ils travaillent sérieusement = They work seriously

Ask: How does she sing? → Beautifully (adverb)

Function 2: Modifying an Adjective

Adverbs show the intensity or degree of an adjective:

Il est très intelligent = He is very intelligent
La maison est assez grande = The house is quite large

Purpose: The adverb (very, quite) emphasizes the adjective's meaning.

Function 3: Modifying Another Adverb

Adverbs can modify other adverbs:

Elle parle assez rapidement = She speaks quite quickly
Ils travaillent très sérieusement = They work very seriously

Function 4: Modifying an Entire Sentence

Some adverbs express an opinion about the whole sentence:

Malheureusement, il est absent = Unfortunately, he is absent
Heureusement, tout va bien = Fortunately, everything is fine

PART 2: TYPES OF ADVERBS

Type 1: ADVERBS OF MANNER (How?)

These answer the question "How?" or "In what manner?"

Common Adverbs of Manner:

  • bien (well)
  • mal (badly)
  • doucement (gently, softly)
  • rapidement (quickly)
  • facilement (easily)
  • difficilement (with difficulty)
  • sérieusement (seriously)
  • poliment (politely)
  • prudemment (carefully)
  • paresseusement (lazily)

Examples:

Il travaille sérieusement = He works seriously (How does he work?)
Elle répond poliment = She answers politely (How does she answer?)
Ils ont terminé le travail rapidement = They finished the work quickly (How did they finish?)
Parlez doucement, s'il vous plaît = Speak softly, please
Il conduit prudemment = He drives carefully

KEY RULE: Adverbs of manner typically follow the verb in French.

Type 2: ADVERBS OF TIME (When?)

These answer the question "When?" or "At what time?"

Common Adverbs of Time:

  • aujourd'hui (today)
  • hier (yesterday)
  • demain (tomorrow)
  • avant-hier (the day before yesterday)
  • après-demain (the day after tomorrow)
  • maintenant (now)
  • bientôt (soon)
  • déjà (already)
  • tard (late - no specific scheduled time)
  • en retard (late - after a scheduled time)
  • toujours (always)
  • jamais (never)
  • autrefois (formerly)
  • récemment (recently)
  • prochainement (next/soon)
  • actuellement (currently)
  • immédiatement (immediately)
  • ensuite (then, next)
  • enfin (finally)

Examples:

Je travaille aujourd'hui = I'm working today (When?)
Il vient demain = He's coming tomorrow (When?)
Nous sommes arrivés hier = We arrived yesterday (When?)
Elle danse toujours à cette heure = She's always dancing at this time
Ils ont enfin trouvé une solution = They finally found a solution
Il pleut maintenant = It's raining now

Important Distinction:

tard = late (no reference to a specific time)
Il est rentré tard dans la nuit = He came home late at night
(No specific expected time)

en retard = late (after an expected/scheduled time)
Je suis toujours en retard = I'm always late
(For example, when work starts at 8:00 and you arrive at 8:10)

KEY RULE: In past tense, adverbs of time often come between the auxiliary verb and the past participle:
Il a déjà mangé = He has already eaten (not "Il a mangé déjà")

Type 3: ADVERBS OF PLACE (Where?)

These answer the question "Where?" or "In what location?"

Common Adverbs of Place:

  • ici (here)
  • là (there)
  • là-bas (over there)
  • ailleurs (elsewhere)
  • partout (everywhere)
  • nulle part (nowhere)
  • quelque part (somewhere)
  • dehors (outside)
  • dedans (inside)
  • devant (in front)
  • derrière (behind)
  • près (near)
  • loin (far)
  • autour (around)
  • dessus (above/on top)
  • dessous (below/underneath)

With Prepositions (modify meaning):

  • auprès de (close to)
  • au-dessus de (above)
  • au-dessous de (below)
  • à côté de (beside, next to)
  • près de (near to)

Examples:

Elle habite ailleurs = She lives elsewhere (Where?)
Les enfants jouent dehors = The children play outside (Where?)
L'école est située près du centre-ville = The school is located near downtown (Where?)
Mes enfants jouent ici = My children are playing here (Where?)
La boulangerie est à côté de la maison = The bakery is beside the house (Where?)
Je cherche mon livre ici, mais il doit être ailleurs = I'm looking for my book here, but it must be somewhere else
Elle habite loin = She lives far away

KEY RULE: Adverbs of place typically come after the verb.

PART 3: FORMATION OF ADVERBS OF MANNER

The Basic Formula: Feminine Adjective + -ment

Most adverbs of manner are formed by:

  1. Taking the feminine form of the adjective
  2. Adding -ment
Masculine Feminine Adverb Meaning
lent lente lentement slowly
rapide rapide rapidement quickly
heureux heureuse heureusement happily
sérieux sérieuse sérieusement seriously
facile facile facilement easily
difficile difficile difficilement with difficulty
général générale généralement generally
naturel naturelle naturellement naturally

Shortcut: Adjectives Ending in -E

If the adjective already ends in -e, simply add -ment:

simple → simplement (simply)
vrai → vraiment (truly)
probable → probablement (probably)

Special Cases (Exceptions exist!)

Some adverbs have irregular formations or unique forms:

bon (good) → bien (well) - NOT "bonnement"
mauvais (bad) → mal (badly) - NOT "mauvaisement"
meilleur (better) → mieux (better) - NOT "meilleurement"

PART 4: PLACEMENT OF ADVERBS

General Rules

Adverbs of Manner:

Usually come AFTER the verb:

Il parle lentement = He speaks slowly
Elle marche rapidement = She walks quickly

Adverbs of Time & Place:

Can come at the beginning or end of a sentence:

Aujourd'hui, je travaille = Today, I work
Je travaille aujourd'hui = I work today
Hier, nous sommes allés au restaurant = Yesterday, we went to a restaurant

In Past Tense:

Adverbs of time often come between auxiliary and past participle:

Il a déjà mangé = He has already eaten (Better than "Il a mangé déjà")
Nous avons enfin trouvé une solution = We finally found a solution

Function Describes NOUNS Modifies VERBS, ADJECTIVES, ADVERBS, SENTENCES

🏆 The Golden Rule: When you see a word after a verb, ask "What question does it answer?"
HOW? → Adverb of manner
WHEN? → Adverb of time
WHERE? → Adverb of place
WHAT QUALITY? → Adjective (but this would need the verb "to be")
