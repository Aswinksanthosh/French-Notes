Grammar: Accents

French uses 4 accent marks that change pronunciation:

1. Accent aigu (é) — acute accent, sounds like "ay"
Example: é in "café" = kah-FAY

2. Accent grave (è) — grave accent, sounds like "eh" (more open)
Example: è in "mère" = MEHR

3. Cédille (ç) — soft 's' sound before a/o/u
Example: ç in "français" = franss-AY (not frank-AY)

4. Tréma (ï) — keeps two vowels as separate sounds
Example: maïs (corn) — mah-EE (not rhymes with "but")

French uses accent marks that change pronunciation.

Grammar: Subject Pronouns

Subject pronouns are words that replace a noun when they do an action or are at the beginning of a sentence.

For example: Instead of saying "Jean is tall" → we say "He is tall"

In French, we have 6 subject pronouns: each one changes depending on who does the action and affects how we conjugate verbs.

This is one of the most important concepts in French!

1. Je (I) — Used when talking about yourself
Example: Je suis étudiant = I am a student

2. Tu (you - informal) — With friends, family
Example: Tu es gentil = You are kind

3. Il / Elle (he / she) — Il for boys, Elle for girls
Example: Il est français = He is French

4. Nous (we) — For groups
Example: Nous sommes amis = We are friends

5. Vous (you - formal/plural) — Teachers, bosses, plural
Example: Vous êtes professeur = You are a teacher

6. Ils / Elles (they) — Ils for boys/mixed, Elles for girls
Example: Ils sont étudiants = They are students

The subject pronoun comes at the beginning of a sentence before the verb. It tells us WHO does the action.

Examples:

Je parle = I speak
Tu danses = You dance
Elle chante = She sings
Nous courons = We run

Important: The verb changes based on the pronoun used. This is called conjugation.

Memorize how each verb changes for each pronoun!

Tu vs Vous (The Most Important Distinction!):

Tu = Informal (You with friends/family)

Used with: friends, family, classmates, people your age
Creates a friendly, personal atmosphere
Example: Tu es mon ami = You are my friend (to a close friend)

Vous = Formal/Respectful (You with strangers/authority)

Used with: teachers, boss, grandparents, strangers, people you want to show respect to
Also used when talking to multiple people (even friends!)
Example: Vous êtes professeur = You are a teacher (to your teacher)

⚠️ Cultural Note: Using Tu with someone you just met can seem rude. Using Vous with close friends can seem too formal. Getting this right is crucial in French society!

Endings — Foundational

Topic: How to identify noun gender in French by word endings, special cases, and common exceptions.

Why Noun Gender Matters

Every French noun has a gender: masculine or feminine.

This gender affects:

  • Which article you use (le vs la, un vs une)
  • How adjectives agree (grand vs grande)
  • How possessives work (mon vs ma, ton vs ta)
  • Past participles in compound tenses

The gender is often unpredictable, BUT there are patterns! Most word endings follow gender rules that can help you guess correctly.

Feminine Noun Endings

Most common feminine endings:

  • -tion / -ion: Almost ALWAYS feminine
    Examples: la connexion, une option, une décision, une nation, la station
  • -ure: Usually feminine
    Examples: la nature, la voiture, la culture, la couture, la peinture
  • -ade: Usually feminine
    Examples: la promenade, la salade, la cascade, la marmelade
  • -e: Most words ending in -e are feminine
    Examples: la porte, la maison, la vie, la page, la table

Masculine Noun Endings

Most common masculine endings:

  • -ment: Usually masculine
    Examples: le gouvernement, le département, le jugement, le logement, le traitement
  • -age: Usually masculine
    Examples: le village, le fromage, le voyage, le courage, le garage, le bagage
  • -oir: Usually masculine
    Examples: le miroir, le tiroir, le couloir, le devoir
  • -isme: Usually masculine
    Examples: le tourisme, le journalisme, le romantisme, le socialisme

Special Cases: Words Borrowed from English, Days, Months & Seasons

Words borrowed from English = MASCULINE

  • le week-end (weekend)
  • le parking (parking lot)
  • le sport (sport)
  • le tennis (tennis)
  • le football (soccer)

Days of the week = MASCULINE

  • le lundi (Monday), le mardi (Tuesday), le mercredi (Wednesday)
  • le jeudi (Thursday), le vendredi (Friday)
  • le samedi (Saturday), le dimanche (Sunday)

Months = MASCULINE

  • le janvier, le février, le mars, le avril, le mai, le juin
  • le juillet, le août, le septembre, le octobre, le novembre, le décembre

Seasons = MASCULINE

  • le printemps (spring), l'été (summer)
  • l'automne (autumn), l'hiver (winter)

Common Exceptions: Masculine Words Ending in -e

Masculine words ending in -e (common exceptions):

  • Family: le père (father), le frère (brother)
  • People: le philosophe (philosopher), le poète (poet), l'artiste (artist - can be either)
  • Places: le musée (museum), le lycée (high school), le café (café)
  • Other: le tableau (painting), le carnaval (carnival)

⚠️ Strategy: When learning a new noun, always memorize it with its article (le or la, un or une). Don't rely only on word endings!

Articles — Thursday, July 2, 2026

Topic: Definite articles (le/la/l'/les), indefinite articles (un/une/des), and partitive articles (du/de la/de l'/des). Note: Master noun gender from the previous lesson first!

About Articles and Gender

Articles MUST match noun gender and number.

Example masculine noun: le village (the village) / un village (a village)

Example feminine noun: la ville (the city) / une ville (a city)

You already learned how to identify gender by word endings. Now apply that knowledge to choose the right article!

Definite Articles (le / la / l' / les)

What are definite articles?

Definite articles mean "the" in English and must match the gender (masculine or feminine) and number (singular or plural) of the noun:

  • LE — masculine singular (le village)
  • LA — feminine singular (la ville)
  • L' — before vowels or mute h (l'homme, l'amie)
  • LES — plural of any gender (les villages, les villes)

Indefinite Articles (un / une / des)

What are indefinite articles?

Indefinite articles mean "a" or "an" in English and refer to unspecified nouns. They must match gender and number:

  • UN — masculine singular (un village)
  • UNE — feminine singular (une ville)
  • DES — plural of any gender (des villages, des amies)

What are partitive articles?

Partitive articles mean "some" or "any" and express an unspecified quantity. They're formed by combining 'de' with the definite article:

What is liaison?

Grammar: What is an Adverb? (Complete Definition)

Adverbs are invariable words (never change). They modify verbs (how?), adjectives (how much?), other adverbs (how much?), or whole sentences (what's your opinion?). This is the key difference from adjectives.

An adverb is an invariable word (NEVER CHANGES, no gender or number agreement) that modifies or clarifies:

  • A VERB — Describes HOW an action is done: Il parle lentement (How does he speak? Slowly)
  • An ADJECTIVE — Describes the DEGREE or INTENSITY: Il est très beau (How beautiful? Very)
  • Another ADVERB — Modifies another adverb: Il parle très lentement (How slowly? Very slowly)
  • The WHOLE SENTENCE — Expresses an opinion about the entire idea: Malheureusement, il a raté l'examen (Unfortunately, he failed the exam)

⚠️ NUANCED DISTINCTIONS & CONFUSING PAIRS

Many students confuse: aussi (casual) vs également (formal), rater (YOU miss) vs manquer (THING is missing from you), tard (general late) vs en retard (scheduled time late), personne (nobody with ne) vs une personne (somebody). These are critical for sounding natural in French!

Distinction 1: "Aussi" vs "Également" (Also/Too)

Both mean "also" but have subtle differences in usage:

aussialso, tooMore casual, general. Can mean "therefore" in older texts. Very common.Marie est venue. Son frère aussi. (Marie came. Her brother too.)

Distinction 2: "Rater" vs "Manquer" (To miss) — CRITICAL DIFFERENCE!

This is a common mistake! These two verbs are NOT interchangeable. Different structures, different meanings:

VerbMeaning/UsageSubject of SentenceExamples

  • Rater = YOU make a mistake, YOU fail, YOU don't get there in time

Distinction 4: "Tarde" (Adverb of time)

Tard means "late" but WITHOUT a specific reference time. It's vague:

Le bus est en retard (The bus is late — after its scheduled time)

🎯 DEFINING PREPOSITIONS

Definition: An invariable word that introduces a complement and establishes a relationship between sentence elements. Examples: Je vais à Paris (I go to Paris — preposition "à" shows destination).

A preposition is an invariable word (NEVER CHANGES) that shows the relationship between elements of a sentence:

  • PLACE: Je suis à l'école (I am at school — preposition "à" shows location)
  • TIME: Le cours commence à 8 heures (The class starts at 8 o'clock — preposition "à" shows time)
  • MEANS: Je vais au travail en bus (I go to work by bus — preposition "en" shows transportation method)
  • ORIGIN: Je viens de France (I come from France — preposition "de" shows origin)
