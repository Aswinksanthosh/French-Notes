Grammar: Accents

French uses accent marks that change pronunciation.
Accent aigu (é) — acute accent.
Accent grave (è) — grave accent.
Cédille (ç) — turns a "c" before a/o/u into a soft "s" sound instead of a hard "k" sound.
Tréma (ï) — shows two vowels next to each other are pronounced separately, not blended into one sound.

français — the cédille makes this "franss-ay", not "frank-ay".
François — a common first name using the same cédille.
maïs (corn) vs mais (but) — the tréma on maïs keeps the "a" and "i" as two separate sounds; without it, "mais" is one blended sound meaning "but".

Grammar: Subject Pronouns

Subject pronouns are words that replace a noun when they do an action or are at the beginning of a sentence.

For example: Instead of saying "Jean is tall" → we say "He is tall"

In French, we have 6 subject pronouns: each one changes depending on who does the action and affects how we conjugate verbs.

This is one of the most important concepts in French!

1. Je (I) — Used when talking about yourself
Example: Je suis étudiant = I am a student

2. Tu (you - informal) — With friends, family
Example: Tu es gentil = You are kind

3. Il / Elle (he / she) — Il for boys, Elle for girls
Example: Il est français = He is French

4. Nous (we) — For groups
Example: Nous sommes amis = We are friends

5. Vous (you - formal/plural) — Teachers, bosses, plural
Example: Vous êtes professeur = You are a teacher

6. Ils / Elles (they) — Ils for boys/mixed, Elles for girls
Example: Ils sont étudiants = They are students

The subject pronoun comes at the beginning of a sentence before the verb. It tells us WHO does the action.

Examples:

Je parle = I speak
Tu danses = You dance
Elle chante = She sings
Nous courons = We run

Important: The verb changes based on the pronoun used. This is called conjugation.

Memorize how each verb changes for each pronoun!

Tu vs Vous (The Most Important Distinction!):

Tu = Informal (You with friends/family)

Used with: friends, family, classmates, people your age
Creates a friendly, personal atmosphere
Example: Tu es mon ami = You are my friend (to a close friend)

Vous = Formal/Respectful (You with strangers/authority)

Used with: teachers, boss, grandparents, strangers, people you want to show respect to
Also used when talking to multiple people (even friends!)
Example: Vous êtes professeur = You are a teacher (to your teacher)

⚠️ Cultural Note: Using Tu with someone you just met can seem rude. Using Vous with close friends can seem too formal. Getting this right is crucial in French society!

Definite Articles (le / la / l' / les)

What are definite articles?

Definite articles mean "the" in English and must match the gender (masculine or feminine) and number (singular or plural) of the noun:

  • LE — masculine singular (le village)
  • LA — feminine singular (la ville)
  • L' — before vowels or mute h (l'homme, l'amie)
  • LES — plural of any gender (les villages, les villes)

Examples: Definite articles

Indefinite Articles (un / une / des)

What are indefinite articles?

Indefinite articles mean "a" or "an" in English and refer to unspecified nouns. They must match gender and number:

  • UN — masculine singular (un village)
  • UNE — feminine singular (une ville)
  • DES — plural of any gender (des villages, des amies)

Use indefinite articles when mentioning something for the first time or when the noun is not specific.

Partitive Articles (some / any)

What are partitive articles?

Partitive articles mean "some" or "any" and express an unspecified quantity. They're formed by combining 'de' with the definite article:

  • DU — de + le (du pain — some bread)
  • DE LA — de + la (de la viande — some meat)
  • DE L' — de + l' (de l'eau — some water)
  • DES — de + les (des haricots — some beans)

Use partitive articles for uncountable nouns and when referring to an unspecified amount.

Je voudrais du pain. — I would like some bread.
Elle mange du chocolat. — She eats (some) chocolate.
Nous buvons de l'eau minérale. — We drink (some) mineral water.
Il achète de la viande. — He is buying (some) meat.
Elle fait pousser des haricots verts. — She grows green beans.

Liaison (linking sounds)

What is liaison?

Liaison is the pronunciation of a normally-silent consonant when the next word starts with a vowel or mute h. Examples:

  • un Américain — the "n" links to "Américain"
  • les Allemands — the "s" sounds like "z" (lez-Allemands)
  • les amis — liaison happens (lez-ami)
  • les villages — NO liaison (village starts with consonant)

Liaison creates smooth connections between words.

Grammar: What is an Adverb? (Complete Definition)

Adverbs are invariable words (never change). They modify verbs (how?), adjectives (how much?), other adverbs (how much?), or whole sentences (what's your opinion?). This is the key difference from adjectives.

An adverb is an invariable word (NEVER CHANGES, no gender or number agreement) that modifies or clarifies:

  • A VERB — Describes HOW an action is done: Il parle lentement (How does he speak? Slowly)
  • An ADJECTIVE — Describes the DEGREE or INTENSITY: Il est très beau (How beautiful? Very)
  • Another ADVERB — Modifies another adverb: Il parle très lentement (How slowly? Very slowly)
  • The WHOLE SENTENCE — Expresses an opinion about the entire idea: Malheureusement, il a raté l'examen (Unfortunately, he failed the exam)

Example 2 (Adverb): Elle chante joliment (She sings prettily — adverb describes verb)

Joliment is an adverb modifying the verb "chante" — it's invariable.

Example 3 (Adjective): C'est un homme intelligent (He's an intelligent man — adjective)

Intelligent agrees with the masculine noun "homme."

Example 4 (Adverb): Il parle intelligemment (He speaks intelligently — adverb)

Intelligemment modifies the verb "parle" and never changes.

Example 5 (Both): Une femme rapide court rapidement (A fast woman runs fast — both forms!)

Rapide (adjective) describes the woman; rapidement (adverb) describes how she runs.

❌ COMMON MISTAKE: Students try to use adverbs to modify nouns. WRONG!

❌ Un homme heureusement — INCORRECT (can't say "a happily man")
✓ Un homme heureux — CORRECT (a happy man) — adjective
✓ Il est heureux — CORRECT (He is happy) — adjective after linking verb
✓ Il sourit heureusement — CORRECT (He smiles happily) — adverb

Grammar: Complete Types of Adverbs (7 Categories)

1️⃣ Adverbs of MANNER — "Comment ?" (How is it done?)

Definition: These describe the WAY or MANNER in which an action is performed.

Key list (most common):

bien (well), mal (badly), lentement (slowly), rapidement (quickly), facilement (easily), sérieusement (seriously), poliment (politely), prudemment (carefully), paresseusement (lazily), prudemment (prudently)

Usage examples from class practice:

Il parle lentement (He speaks slowly) — describes HOW he speaks
Elle travaille beaucoup (She works a lot) — describes HOW MUCH she works
Je mange le gâteau doucement (I eat cake slowly) — describes HOW I eat
Elle conduit rapidement (She drives quickly) — describes HOW she drives
Il a bien travaillé (He worked well) — describes QUALITY of work

2️⃣ Adverbs of TIME — "Quand ?" (When?)

Definition: These indicate WHEN an action occurs or has occurred.

Extended list:

aujourd'hui (today), hier (yesterday), demain (tomorrow), avant-hier (day before yesterday), maintenant (now), bientôt (soon), déjà (already), récemment (recently), enfin (finally)

Real-life examples from class:

Aujourd'hui, je travaille (Today, I work) — starting the sentence
J'ai déjà mangé (I already ate) — between auxiliary & participle in passé composé
Il dîne toujours avant 19h (He always dines before 7 PM) — after verb
Je vais souvent au marché (I often go to the market) — after verb
Elle est toujours ponctuelle (She is always punctual) — modifying adjective

⚠️ IMPORTANT DISTINCTION:

tard — late (vague, no specific time reference). Example: Je mange tard (I eat late — no specific time)
en retard — late (after an expected/scheduled time). Example: Je suis en retard au travail (I'm late for work — after my usual time)

3️⃣ Adverbs of PLACE — "Où ?" (Where?)

Definition: These indicate WHERE an action happens or WHERE something is located.

Complete list:

ici (here), là (there), là-bas (over there), ailleurs (elsewhere), partout (everywhere), nulle part (nowhere), dehors (outside), dedans (inside), devant (in front), derrière (behind), près (near), loin (far)

Classroom practice examples:

Les enfants jouent dehors (The children play outside) — describes WHERE they play
Il y a beaucoup d'invités dans la fête (There are many guests at the party) — WHERE the guests are
J'habite où ? — J'habite ici (Where do I live? — I live here) — location answer
Ma maison est près de la gare (My house is near the station) — position/location
Elle vient souvent ici (She comes here often) — WHERE she comes + FREQUENCY

📖 Grammar: Formation of Adverbs of Manner (THE RULE)

The formation rule is simple: FEMININE ADJECTIVE + -ment = ADVERB. We use the feminine form because it usually has a vowel ending, making it easier to add -ment. Some adverbs like bien (well) and mal (badly) don't follow this pattern because they're independent words.

🔑 THE KEY RULE: Feminine Adjective + -ment = Adverb

To form most adverbs from adjectives, take the FEMININE FORM of the adjective and add -ment. This gives you the adverb.

WHY the feminine? Because it's a pattern: The feminine form often has a vowel sound at the end that makes it easy to add -ment.

4️⃣ Adverbs of QUANTITY & INTENSITY — "À quel degré ?" (To what degree? How much?)

Definition: These indicate the AMOUNT or DEGREE of intensity of something.

Complete list:

très (very), trop (too much), assez (enough/quite), beaucoup (a lot/much), plus (more), moins (less), presque (almost), tellement (so much), vraiment (really), complètement (completely), à peine (barely)

Key examples showing usage:

Il est très grand (He is VERY tall) — modifying adjective, indicating degree
C'est trop difficile (It's TOO difficult) — showing excess
J'adore beaucoup ma famille (I LOVE a lot my family) — indicating amount of feeling
Nous sommes presque prêts (We are ALMOST ready) — nearly complete
C'est assez facile (It's QUITE easy) — sufficient degree
Il parle tellement (He speaks SO MUCH) — degree of action
C'est vraiment important (It's REALLY important) — emphasis on importance

⚠️ PLACEMENT RULE FOR QUANTITY/INTENSITY: These adverbs come BEFORE adjectives and adverbs, NOT after:

✓ Il est très beau (He is very handsome)
✗ Il est beau très — WRONG
✓ Elle parle très lentement (She speaks very slowly)
✗ Elle parle lentement très — WRONG

5️⃣ Adverbs of FREQUENCY — "Combien de fois ?" (How often?)

Definition: These indicate HOW OFTEN or HOW REGULARLY an action happens (frequency spectrum from always to never).

Frequency scale (most frequent → least frequent):

toujours (always) → souvent (often) → généralement (generally) → habituellement (usually) → normalement (normally) → parfois (sometimes) → rarement (rarely) → presque jamais (almost never) → jamais (never)

Real classroom practice examples:

Je vais souvent au marché (I go to the market OFTEN) — regularity of action
Elle est toujours ponctuelle (She is ALWAYS punctual) — consistent quality
Nous mangeons rarement au restaurant (We RARELY eat at restaurants) — infrequent action
Il ne fume jamais (He NEVER smokes) — zero frequency
Je dîne toujours avant 19h (I always have dinner before 7 PM) — consistent habit

⚠️ CRITICAL: "Jamais" as NEGATION ADVERB (NOT just frequency)

Jamais often pairs with ne to create negation in French. This is different from English "never" which stands alone:

Structure: ne + VERB + jamais (or ne + ADVERB + jamais)

Il ne fume jamais (He NEVER smokes) — negation of smoking
Je ne prends jamais le bus (I never take the bus) — negation of taking bus
Il n'a jamais visité la France (He has never visited France) — negation in past
Je ne mange jamais de viande (I never eat meat) — negation with partitive

⚠️ NOTE: Jamais CANNOT stand alone as a positive statement. Always needs ne for negation. Without ne, the meaning would be "ever" in questions.

🎯 CRITICAL: Placement of Adverbs (THE MOST IMPORTANT SECTION)

Adverb placement depends on TENSE and ADVERB TYPE: Simple tenses = AFTER verb. Passé composé SHORT adverbs = between aux & participle. Passé composé LONG adverbs = after participle. Intensity adverbs = BEFORE adjectives. This is the most important rule!

This is where many students make mistakes! Adverb placement changes based on TENSE and ADVERB TYPE.

⚠️ NUANCED DISTINCTIONS & CONFUSING PAIRS

Many students confuse: aussi (casual) vs également (formal), rater (YOU miss) vs manquer (THING is missing from you), tard (general late) vs en retard (scheduled time late), personne (nobody with ne) vs une personne (somebody). These are critical for sounding natural in French!

Distinction 1: "Aussi" vs "Également" (Also/Too)

Both mean "also" but have subtle differences in usage:

WordMeaningNuanceExample
aussialso, tooMore casual, general. Can mean "therefore" in older texts. Very common.Marie est venue. Son frère aussi. (Marie came. Her brother too.)
J'aime les films. Moi aussi. (I like films. Me too.)
égalementalso, equally, likewiseMore formal, more emphatic. Emphasizes equality or parallel ideas. Academic feel.Marie est venue. Son frère également. (Marie came. Her brother likewise.)
Les parents aussi, équilibre également important. (Parents also, balance equally important.)

Distinction 2: "Rater" vs "Manquer" (To miss) — CRITICAL DIFFERENCE!

This is a common mistake! These two verbs are NOT interchangeable. Different structures, different meanings:

VerbMeaning/UsageSubject of SentenceExamples
raterMiss an opportunity, bus, plane, OR fail an exam. The subject makes a mistake or fails.The PERSON who missed/failedJ'ai raté le bus (I MISSED the bus — I wasn't there in time)
Il a raté l'avion (He MISSED the flight)
J'ai raté l'examen (I FAILED the exam — I did poorly)
Il a raté son examen (He failed his test)
manquerMiss someone/something (person misses you; thing is absent). Shows absence or lack.What is MISSING or who is MISSED (often reversed in English)Tu me manques (I miss you — literally "You are missing from me")
Il manque du sel dans la sauce (Salt is missing in the sauce — salt is the subject)

Ma mère me manque (I miss my mother)

🔑 THE KEY DIFFERENCE:

  • Rater = YOU make a mistake, YOU fail, YOU don't get there in time
  • Manquer = Something is ABSENT, someone is NOT THERE (you miss them)

Distinction 3: "Personne" (Nobody) — Context Matters!

Same word, completely different meaning depending on context:

Distinction 4: "Tarde" (Adverb of time)

Tard means "late" but WITHOUT a specific reference time. It's vague:

Je me couche tard (I go to bed late — no specific time, just generally late)

En retard means "late" with reference to an EXPECTED time — you're behind schedule:

Je suis en retard au travail (I'm late for work — after my usual arrival time)
Le bus est en retard (The bus is late — after its scheduled time)

📋 PRACTICE TIPS (From Classroom Teaching)

✅ DO THIS:

  • Always learn adverbs IN COMPLETE SENTENCES, never isolated words. Your brain learns context, not just vocabulary.
  • Practice using adverbs actively in sentences you create. Say them out loud, write them down, use them in context.
  • For each adverb, ask yourself: "What question does it answer?" This becomes automatic with practice.
  • Pay attention to placement! Make sure your adverb is in the right position for the tense.
  • Use adverbs in conversations. Logical connectors (pourtant, cependant, etc.) make you sound fluent!

❌ DON'T DO THIS:

  • ❌ Don't memorize adverbs as lists without context
  • ❌ Don't put adverbs in wrong positions — placement matters!
  • ❌ Don't confuse rater/manquer or autre/également
  • ❌ Don't use adverbs to modify nouns (that's what adjectives do)
  • ❌ Don't forget that adverbs are INVARIABLE — they never change form
